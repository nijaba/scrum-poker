Estimate your Jira backlog together, with cards face down.

A multiplayer scrum poker app for Jira Cloud. It walks your team through the tickets that still need an estimate, in your backlog's order. Everyone votes in secret, the cards turn over together, and the agreed story points are written back to Jira.

How a session runs

  1. 1

    Open the room

    A facilitator signs in with Google or GitHub. Their room is connected to a Jira board, and the queue fills with the backlog tickets that have no estimate yet.

  2. 2

    Share the link

    Players open the room link and type a name. No account, nothing to install, on a laptop or a phone.

  3. 3

    Vote in secret

    Everyone picks a card. The table shows who has voted, never what: not even the facilitator sees a card before the reveal.

  4. 4

    Agree and move on

    The cards turn over together. The team talks about the spread, the facilitator confirms the points, and they are written to the ticket in Jira.

Why cards face down?

When people estimate out loud, the first number said pulls every other number towards it. Secret cards let each person commit to their own view first, so the quiet voices count as much as the loud ones.

A 2 next to a 13 is the most useful thing a reveal can show: two people picture different work. Talking it through before the sprint is far cheaper than finding out halfway through it. More on why it works

What it does

  • Your backlog, in order

    Tickets come from the board's backlog in Jira's rank order. Epics, sub-tasks and tickets in a sprint stay out; each room can tune its queue filter and add JQL.

  • Points go back to Jira

    Confirmed estimates are written through the board's own estimation field. The room only moves on once Jira has accepted the write.

  • Your scale

    Fibonacci, T-shirt sizes mapped to points, or your own list of cards.

  • A countdown that keeps it moving

    An optional timer, with a soft tick at the end. It signals time's up and never reveals on its own. Auto-reveal turns the cards once everyone has voted.

  • Phones and dark mode

    A compact phone layout with the hand docked at the bottom, a remote view for players who watch a shared screen, and a dark palette that follows the device.

  • Facilitators you choose

    The owner names delegates who can run sessions, by Google email or GitHub login, never by display name.

  • A guided tour

    First-timers are offered a short tour of the room, shaped by their role. It is always one click away in the menu.

  • A summary of the session

    Facilitators see what was estimated or skipped, with how the votes fell for each ticket.

Small by design

  • One container, one process. No database server, cache or queue.
  • No votes kept. Votes, results and session counters live in memory only. One SQLite file holds the room definitions.
  • Python and Svelte. FastAPI and WebSockets on the server, a small Svelte app in the browser.
  • HTTPS only, behind your own reverse proxy.
  • Open source, under the Apache License 2.0.

Run your own

docker build -t scrum-poker .

Then register a Google or GitHub sign-in and an Atlassian app, and set a few environment variables. The admin guide walks through it, with Caddy or nginx in front.
